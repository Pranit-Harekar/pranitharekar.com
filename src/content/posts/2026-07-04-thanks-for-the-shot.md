Ten years ago, I stepped off the plane at JFK with a couple of bags, a
scholarship, and honestly, no real clue what I was doing. All I had was a student
visa and a quiet promise to myself to work as hard as I possibly could.

Right from the start, the rules felt different. Nobody cared about my pedigree or
where I came from. They just wanted to know what I could actually bring to the
table. That realization stuck with me instantly. This was a place where your
effort mattered so much more than your background.

Fast forward a decade, and I've built a career that my younger self wouldn't have
even known how to dream up. I've been fortunate to work alongside people who cared
deeply about the quality of my work, never the accent I said it in. There have been
plenty of times I found myself in rooms with people vastly more qualified than me,
but I knew I belonged there because I had earned my seat. That's the beauty of a
culture that rewards effort over legacy or handouts.

As America celebrates its 250th anniversary, I find myself incredibly grateful for
the small chapter I get to write in its ongoing story. I wasn't born into this
nation, but I was welcomed into it. And I am profoundly thankful for the
foundational pillars that made my journey possible: freedom, fairness, and the
unwavering belief that if you're willing to put in the work, you can build
something real here.

Happy 250th, America. Thanks for the shot.
