For years the game is simple. Spot the bug, fix it. Write the cleaner
architecture, ship it. Bring the data, win the argument. Technical accuracy is
your currency, and early on it buys you a lot of wins.

Then you move into a lead role, and the same playbook stops working.

Your best proposals sit in limbo. A refactor you know is needed keeps getting
shelved. And the frustrating part is the ideas aren't wrong. They're just not
landing.

That was the shock for me: technical correctness doesn't automatically buy you
organizational buy-in. Nobody tells you this. You spend years being rewarded for
being right, and then one day being right stops being enough.

## You're not just compiling code anymore

At a certain level, the problem in front of you isn't a compiler error. It's a
human system. Shifting business priorities. Tight timelines. Stakeholders who
are quietly anxious about something they can't quite name. Team dynamics that
predate you by three reorgs.

You can be completely correct about the technology and still lose, because the
decision was never really about the technology.

Real engineering leadership isn't about being the smartest person in the room.
It's influence over correctness. And that stung to learn, because "smartest
person in the room" was the whole identity I'd been optimizing for.

## Questions I ask now when a good idea gets pushed back

- Am I trying to prove I'm right, or trying to move the business forward? Those
  are not always the same thing, and I've confused them plenty.
- Have I framed this in a way a non-technical stakeholder actually cares about?
  "It's cleaner" is not a business reason.
- Can I explain the trade-offs plainly, without getting defensive the second
  someone pokes at them?

Brilliant code that never ships solves exactly zero real-world problems. Lead
engineers don't win by having the best ideas. They win by getting good ideas
across the finish line.

Still working on it, honestly. If you've made this jump, I'm curious: what was
the hardest non-technical skill you had to learn along the way?
