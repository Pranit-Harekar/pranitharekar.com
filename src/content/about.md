I'm a lead software engineer at [Global Payments](https://www.globalpayments.com/point-of-sale/retail),
where I help build the retail point-of-sale systems that quietly move money every
time someone taps a card at a checkout counter. It's the kind of software nobody
notices until it breaks — which is exactly why I lose sleep over it.

I care about writing code a team can still read in two years, long after everyone
has forgotten why we wrote it. I'm also all-in on using AI to move faster — fully
aware that I might be handing my future job to the robots. Shooting myself in the
foot? Probably. But at least the foot will be well documented.

Lately I'm learning how to lead the people who make the software, which turns out to
be a very different puzzle than making the software myself. Turns out you can't
refactor a human, and "have you tried turning it off and on again" is frowned upon
in a one-on-one. I'm figuring it out one awkward, well-meaning conversation at a time.

When I'm not staring at a terminal, I'm playing video games — because who isn't. I've
got roughly 10,000 hours in [Valorant](https://playvalorant.com/en-us/), which is either a proud accomplishment or a
cry for help depending on who you ask. My rank does not reflect the hours. We don't
talk about that. Nope!

Honestly, if I weren't a software engineer, I'd be a farmer. I love farms and farm
animals, and one day I want to build a sustainable organic farm for my family and
friends. Fewer merge conflicts, more goats. That's the dream.
